## Shared timing, motion and camera contract

- Keep **server simulation, animation sampling, display refresh and offline film frame rate distinct**. The existing Rust authority uses 20 Hz; 60 fps is a candidate display/animation review rate and 24 fps a candidate film-delivery rate, not guaranteed hardware performance or a slower network clock. A 0.15 s blend corresponds to three 20 Hz ticks, 0.1 s to two, 0.4 s to eight and 0.5 s to ten; visual interpolation still follows real event timestamps rather than rounding every key to a tick.
- Draft “frame 50” means approximately **0.83 s at 60 fps** or **2.08 s at 24 fps** from a zero-indexed start; the intended source rate and whether the event is a sample or interval are missing. The proposed 48-frame Kali clip is 0.8 s at 60 fps or 2.0 s at 24 fps. The Arjuna 0–40 labels require an explicit sampling/interval convention; at 60 fps, frame 40 is about 0.67 s after frame 0. Record release and contact times in seconds and authoritative ticks, not only frame labels.
- A 0.15 s state blend is a starting candidate, not a replacement for action-specific anticipation, recovery or hit timing. Root motion may drive an **offline authored pose study**, but competitive movement, collisions, cooldowns, hit receipts and objectives remain server-owned; a visual clip cannot override the capsule or move a plant. Check retargeting, prediction, correction and rollback before promotion.
- The proposed `0.8` spring stiffness, `0.6` drag and `0.95` bowstring tension omit units, solver, damping, timestep and scale. Treat them as placeholders until those quantities and a stable target frame-rate range are specified; their values cannot simply be copied between engines.
- The draft's 0.4 s slow motion, 15° impact tilt, 30° stagger tilt, desaturation, vignette and chromatic shifts are **optional presentation experiments**. Never slow the shared online simulation, obscure a site, force a 12-second lockout or make a target harder to identify. Test a zero-tilt, no-flash, reduced-motion and no-audio path. An optional offline film may use different editorial timing without changing combat.
- Kalaripayattu and Bharatanatyam are distinct living practices, not interchangeable mocap tags or a license to equate every pose with a scripture. Source specific movement with named practitioners, consent and recorded usage terms; use performers and cultural reviewers where available, then hand-key and evaluate the result. The supplied claim that capture and Sanskrit coaching already occurred is **unverified**. No source recording or coach receipt was supplied here.

The user-supplied damage text `150 + (TargetMaxHP _ 0.15) _ (1 + Dharma/100)` contains ambiguous underscore operators and an undefined Dharma resource. **No formula is accepted or implemented.** As an illustration only, if both underscores mean multiplication, a 300-HP target and Dharma 0 would yield `150 + 300 × 0.15 × 1 = 195`, or 65% of max health before defenses. That example is not the current damage rule or a balance recommendation. The draft's species/tag multipliers and alignment damage likewise need a separately approved ruleset; Ember Rite currently uses team/eligibility checks, not lore labels.

The proposed `800u/s` projectile speed also has no portable meaning. Unreal's documented default distance unit is centimetres, so **if** `u` means cm, 800 cm/s = 8 m/s. In a project convention of one unit per metre, 800 units/s would instead be 800 m/s. Record the actual engine unit and `120°` as either a total angle or an angular rate before implementation; do not silently assume either.

## Performance experiment, not a guarantee

Use the supplied 60k/25k/8k triangle levels, 4k/2k/1k texture tiers and 0–5/5–15/15+ m distances as **initial candidates**. Assess screen size, silhouette pops, shadow and material cost instead of forcing distance cutoffs. Establish whether counts are per hero, all meshes, equipped weapons or effects; count triangles, skin influences, materials, texture residency and draw calls on the exact export.

Likewise, “pool size 200/50/30” could mean particles, emitters, actors or requests. Define capacity, lifetime, reuse/reset, ownership and saturation behavior separately, including ten simultaneous players and five overlapping abilities; then benchmark the chosen engine. Niagara is an Unreal VFX system, not a generic synonym for cloth/fur simulation. Compare authored cards, skeletal secondary motion, cloth/groom or engine-native effects only where the chosen renderer actually supports them.

The proposed **450 MB for five heroes + VFX + animation** is unmeasured until it states CPU/GPU split, resident versus peak memory, shared textures, compression, streaming and the simultaneous hero/skin/effect set. The known laptop profile is an RTX 4070 Laptop GPU with **8 GB dedicated VRAM**; shared memory is not equivalent to local VRAM. Profile a named device, renderer, resolution and quality tier with p50/p95/p99 frame time, peak/resident memory, draw calls and visual evidence. The existing two-rendered-client Sena stall must be revisited; no hardware smoothness is claimed here.

| 6 | **Accessibility + performance:** test normal/reduced settings, both teams, different surfaces and named target hardware. | Real frame-time/memory captures, profiler settings, readable action/objective states without sound or color alone, user feedback and documented failures. |

Candidate motion timing tolerance is **within one 20 Hz tick (50 ms)** between an authoritative event and its intended presentation marker; measure the actual error at each reviewed beat rather than presenting this target as a result. AI-generated illustrations, offline animation studies, authored clips, in-engine assets and actual online playback are separate deliverables and acceptance gates.
