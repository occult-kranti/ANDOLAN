# Reading the Ember Rite districts

On the way into a room, a player needs three answers. Which landmark am I approaching? Where will the next door put me? What can the other team learn about my choice? These readings informed four original spaces, their paper floor plans and a trial for two playable heroes. Source IDs on the architecture sheets refer to the observations below. The dimensions, transport, cover and round economy belong to YUGA.

## Round 1 · Read the places and the evidence

### Mandala Rift · distinguish the tower from the entrance

**M1 · Chola architecture, Tamil Nadu.** UNESCO’s description compares the straight silhouette at Thanjavur with the recessed corners and rising curve of the 53 m sanctum tower at Gangaikondacholisvaram, completed in 1035. At Thanjavur its account separately describes the entrance gateways and the sanctum in the rear half of the court. A *vimana* crowns the sanctum. A *gopura* marks a gateway. For South Pyramid, give the skyline a mass that recedes in tiers and give the actual public passage its own signed, legible opening. “Pyramid” is the map callout for an invented form. The opening provides a fictional fold between circulation spaces. [UNESCO, Great Living Chola Temples, description and brief synthesis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/250/)

**M2 · Settlement and literary landscape.** At Hampi, the description links mandapas, bazaar streets, tanks and water control to the Vijayanagara settlement on the Tungabhadra. A separate literary source gives the banks a human mood. In Griffith’s 1870–74 translation, Rāma by Pampā sees lilies, shaded banks and groves while grieving for Sītā. The reading gives the quiet dry bank a reason to feel different from the working market. Keep the market approach straight enough to read its screen and the bank vegetation low enough to see a crossing. The new Sluice and fold are the game’s geography. [UNESCO, Group of Monuments at Hampi, brief synthesis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/241/) · [Griffith, *Rámáyan*, Kiṣkindhā Kāṇḍa I, lines 37383 onward](https://github.com/GITenberg/The-R-m-yan-of-V-lm-ki-translated-into-English-verse_24869/blob/b48a7a226e8a20bed61131382aa5b8bf2206e039/24869-0.txt#L37383)

### Lanka · sequence the climb and the garden reveal

**L1 · Sigiriya, Sri Lanka.** At the late fifth-century capital of Kassapa I, galleries and stairs connect the slopes and summit of the granite outcrop. The published description makes the access sequence part of the architecture. In Lanka the comparable decision is whether to rise into the Rain Gallery or stay on the low route. Put the next turn in view before a player steps onto the exposed landing. Sigiriya is an inland archaeological site. YUGA supplies the harbour, the shortcut and the epic setting. [UNESCO, Ancient City of Sigiriya, description](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/202/)

**L2 · The search in the Aśoka grove.** Hanumān crosses a boundary, works through shaded alleys, passes water and steps, and climbs a tree to see where Sītā might be. A disturbance wakes a bird. Use the change in what can be seen or heard to distinguish the garden entrance from an open gallery. A player can communicate the crossing they witnessed. A disturbed bird carries no information about an unseen carrier in the game. [Griffith, *Rámáyan*, Sundara Kāṇḍa XIV, lines 46442–46486](https://github.com/GITenberg/The-R-m-yan-of-V-lm-ki-translated-into-English-verse_24869/blob/b48a7a226e8a20bed61131382aa5b8bf2206e039/24869-0.txt#L46442)

### Ayodhya · mark which landing stays dry

**A1 · Rani-ki-Vav, Patan, Gujarat.** The eleventh-century memorial stepwell runs east–west. From the surface, a descending corridor leads past four pavilions of increasing storeys towards a tank and well shaft. The source distinguishes seven levels of stairs from the count of pavilions. Borrow the sequence and the visible change in depth for Measure Well. Give the dry return a broad horizontal landing and repeat the water-edge mark on its lower face so a player looking uphill can find the same exit. The map’s cardinal axis and room dimensions are independently authored. [UNESCO, Rani-ki-Vav, brief synthesis](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/922/)

**A2 · Riverfront use in Ayodhya.** The district describes Ram ki Paidi as a series of ghats along the Saryu used by devotees. That documents a contemporary use of public river steps. In the fictional district the residents also need a safe ferry landing. Preserve a dry spectator/circulation edge around the wash and place the match interaction on its own marked floor. [Government of Uttar Pradesh, District Ayodhya, Ram ki Paidi](https://ayodhya.nic.in/tourist-place/ram-ki-paidi/)

**A3 · City and crossing in the epic.** Ayodhyā in Bāla Kāṇḍa V is placed by the Sarjū, with streets, gates and squares. Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa LII describes Guha arranging a boat at the **Gaṅgā** on Rāma’s journey. Keep the two waterways and episodes distinct. Ferry preparation supplies an intelligible story task for the crew in the game’s imagined ward. It provides no historical coordinates or evidence for the course of the fictional wash. [Griffith, Bāla Kāṇḍa V, lines 1528 onward](https://github.com/GITenberg/The-R-m-yan-of-V-lm-ki-translated-into-English-verse_24869/blob/b48a7a226e8a20bed61131382aa5b8bf2206e039/24869-0.txt#L1528) · [Ayodhyā Kāṇḍa LII, lines 17564 onward](https://github.com/GITenberg/The-R-m-yan-of-V-lm-ki-translated-into-English-verse_24869/blob/b48a7a226e8a20bed61131382aa5b8bf2206e039/24869-0.txt#L17564)

### Kurukshetra · make the record gate readable from either side

**K1 · Dholavira, Gujarat.** The excavated Harappan city on Khadir drew on two seasonal streams. Its walled settlement had separate divisions, multilayered fortifications, stone construction, reservoirs and drainage. This is a comparative precedent for legible thresholds in a settlement dependent on water. On the game’s plains, the Register Gate marks the passage between civic records and field access. The two places have distinct locations, periods and histories. [UNESCO, Dholavira, brief synthesis and criterion iv](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1645/)

**K2 · Direct field evidence from Thanesar, Haryana.** The Archaeological Survey of India published the 1987–88 season at Harsh-ka-Tila in its 1993 review. The team opened trenches in three areas and described about 16 m of occupational deposits. It recorded a substantial brick structure in the Vardhana levels and successive phases of fortification elsewhere. Flat terracotta tablets in one trench had incised lines. The excavators explicitly qualified their stratigraphic position. For YUGA, use courses of brick to make the gate’s return wall legible. The painted level marks and record-keeper’s tally are new motifs. The function of the incisions remains uncertain. Relating the excavated mound to an epic battle layout would need separate evidence. [ASI, *Indian Archaeology 1987–88 — A Review*, §42, printed pp. 28–31, scan p. 36 onward](https://archive.org/download/indianarchaeology198788areview_219_B/Indian%20Archaeology%201987-88%20A%20Review.pdf#page=36)

**K3 · What the watcher knows.** The opening of the *Bhagavad Gītā* locates the question on Kurukshetra. Arjuna later asks Krishna to put his chariot between the sides so he can see who has assembled. The scene turns on a limited observer obtaining information and then reconsidering his action. Let the bridge player see an approach and call *where someone crossed*. The exchange about whether to commit the Ember is original dialogue. [K. M. Ganguli, *Mahabharata*, Bhīṣma Parva, §XXV, *Bhagavad Gītā* I](https://sacred-texts.com/book/the-mahabharata-book-6-bhishma-parva/section-xxv-bhagavad-gita-chapter-i)

## Round 2 · Translate the readings into game decisions

**C1 · Itinerary and measured plan.** In *The History of Cartography*, Joseph E. Schwartzberg reproduces a folio of the *Chahār Gulshan*, a work compiled in 1759–60 and later rearranged. Figure 17.30 reproduces an 1825 transcription. The two-column strip records stages and indicates rivers or hills on the journey. We inspected the plate as well as the caption. Give the technical plan a north arrow and a metre scale. Under a folded-space sheet, read the journey from entrance to threshold, turn and named exit in travel order. A line between remote endpoints indicates a topological connection. Its length on paper cannot be used to calculate walking distance. [University of Chicago Press, vol. 2, book 1, ch. 17, printed pp. 435–436, fig. 17.30, PDF p. 48](https://press.uchicago.edu/books/HOC/HOC_V2_B1/HOC_VOLUME2_Book1_chapter17.pdf#page=48)

**C2 · Redundant symbols.** W3C technique G111 illustrates a transport map that uses different stop symbols along with route colours. Pair the ordinary walk, water crossing and fold with distinct line styles. Repeat portal endpoint IDs at both ends, print the turn sign with its angle, and supply a textual exit/return route. In grayscale, ask a reader to locate the walkable bypass, name the destination and point in the expected direction on arrival. [W3C, G111, description and transport-map example](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/general/G111)

**F1 · Folded rooms and the cost of a shortcut.** Suma and colleagues studied self-overlapping layouts for physical walking in immersive VR. The indexed abstract describes two user studies. The results concern perception in particular headset/room conditions. YUGA uses explicit paired thresholds in a desktop tactical map. Model a fold as an edge of authored cost, with inverse rotation on return. At baseline speed 5 m/s, a 1.5 s crossing costs the time of 7.5 m of uninterrupted level walking. The useful comparison is that cost **plus both approaches** against the full dry bypass. Label facing in both directions and attempt a blocked or interrupted exit in practice. The design makes no claim about curvature, perception thresholds or religious architecture moving people through space. [Suma et al., *Impossible Spaces*, 2012, indexed abstract](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=DOI%3A10.1109%2FTVCG.2012.47&format=json&resultType=core) · [YUGA route measurements](measurements.md)

**G1 · Design the support decision.** Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek describe how information shared among teammates and a goal that is difficult to achieve alone can produce fellowship. Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski report four studies of game play. Perceived competence and autonomy were associated with enjoyment, and the multiplayer survey also examined relatedness. The size of any retention effect in YUGA would need its own study. Give escorts a readable result when they protect a teammate’s ability to carry or quench. In the debrief ask the escort which action they chose, what cue made its effect visible and what they could have done instead. Record actions on separate legal objective events so repetitive button taps cannot become progress. [Hunicke, LeBlanc and Zubek, *MDA*, “Dynamic Models”, PDF p. 3](https://users.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf#page=3) · [Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski, 2006, abstract and studies 1–4](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf)

**G2 · Let expertise and map identity matter.** Riot’s 2019 balance article differentiated player groups and acknowledged that elite win-rate samples can be too small for inference. Its thresholds describe its own production setting. For Ember Rite, record opportunities by hero, side, map and player experience. Hanuman can be the preferred pick for an escorted, close-quarters recovery. Rama can earn the stronger outcome for choosing a clean ranged rotation. Test the preferred pick against an opponent that can force the alternative approach. Track the fraction of *eligible* opportunities converted, plus resource or healing delivered. Small private tests can establish whether the options work. Establishing a competitive meta requires human drafts and a much larger sample. [Riot, *Champion Balance Framework*, 30 May 2019, audience sections](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-champion-balance-framework/)

### Test a situational hero advantage

Hanuman’s Vanara Sena now restores up to 12 HP once to a visible, injured teammate at the field’s arrival, 0.6 s after cast. A 3 m radius, four possible teammates and a 20 s cooldown bound the theoretical result to 48 HP per cast. Against a teammate missing 4 HP, the receipt should report 4. To test G1, give escorts genuine wounded targets on both sides of a wall. Count teammates healed divided by eligible teammates present at impact. Also count protected recoveries and successful quenches separately. The denominator should exclude the caster, full-health teammates and everyone behind cover. [Sena field specification](../../../../../yuga-rust/assets/vanara-sena.json) · [Both-team receiver and receipt assertions](../../../../../yuga-rust/src/battle.rs)

Rama’s step can recover one spent arrow after at least 3 m of collision-resolved displacement. A full magazine and a short, blocked step produce zero recovery. The 12-arrow cap and 5 s cooldown bound sustained recovery to 0.2 arrow/s. In the corridor trial, alternate the sites, sides and corridor order. Report the fraction of spent-arrow steps that complete the required distance and then recover one arrow. A team may prefer Hanuman for a clustered retake or Rama for an open rotation. Human picks, exchanges and win rates by experience band are still needed to determine whether either preference emerges.
