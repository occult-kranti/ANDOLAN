# Kurukshetra · Where the river reaches

At the start of the round the carrier can see a square relief on the palace ridge to the west and three iron prongs above the fields to the east. The middle bridge points at neither. It lands between two small counting houses, where a team can make the other side show its hand before choosing a site. This is a proposed 5v5 Ember Rite map. ## The wager at Gauge Bridge

The court has one surviving wind register. The irrigators have a chalk line left by the last flood. The Confluence preserves the moment before the palace signs an order deciding which fields get water. The **Palace Terrace (A)** stores the seal, and **Wind Field (B)** holds the instrument. Kindling either site commits the reading. Quenching it grants a fresh survey. The river, doors and wind stay fixed throughout play. Reserve the movement of the register’s shadow or the drawing of a new chalk line for the round result.

A scout on **Gauge Bridge** can threaten Seal Porch and Tally Steps in one crossing. A defender seeing that scout learns someone has crossed, while the carrier can still be on either outer bank. One teammate watches the next doorway, another counts the players actually seen. From the outside, the carrier arrives sooner and avoids the bridge, but changing destinations costs a journey back through the hinge. The map gives both teams an imperfect report to act on.

| **Wind Field, +1 m** | A player on Mill Sill can see across the low Threshing Bin. A second arrives at Tally Steps or uses the shallow ford. **Reed Lip** gives the field a fourth entry. Furrow leads along a dry bank to the lip. From there a 5 m-wide shallow shelf loops to the east of B. The proposed wet leg uses a 0.85 movement multiplier in the route model. Its fixed wet pavers repeat at the shore so an entrant can anticipate the change. A listener close to B might hear a wade and still has to see whether the carrier follows. Test an equivalent local caption with sound muted. Keep reeds below knee height, place them outside the path and leave the substrate visible when reflections are reduced. The three dry crossings remain accessible to every hero. Build the first blockout with ordinary walking crossings. Keep the earlier zipline proposal for a separate timed comparison.

## A round the panel can replay

1. During the approach, send a pair to Ox Gate, a scout to Furrow and the carrier with one escort to Boat Yard. The bridge escort calls **“Two seen at Porch.”** The Sill scout calls **“Field clear to bin.”** Neither report settles who is behind Drying Wall.
2. The carrier chooses Tally Steps. The scout holds the Sill entrance while the escort clears the wall. The carrier holds the legal disk for four uninterrupted seconds. If the screen reveals a second defender, release early and regroup with the west pair.
3. On confirmed ignition at B, both teams receive the site and the 40 s clock. A defender who left A via the bridge at that instant would arrive with roughly 18 s left under the walking model. The closer defender can contest the north door or wait for the second player. A premature quench may draw an interrupt and has to start again.
4. A living defender reaches within 1.5 m of the placed vessel and completes a 3.5 s quench, or the fire runs its course. The end card names the round result. No map device identifies the carrier, patrols the river, deals passive damage or changes next round’s terrain. [Authoritative rules experiment and proposed match adapter](../../ROUND_AND_TECH.md)

## Art, sound and geometry checks

- **Orient without a map.** The square Seal Die is cut into A’s parapet. The Flood Rule’s paired notched staffs frame the dry deck at mid. Wind Fork is the fixed, three-pronged casing above the B register. Give the arch and the fork different skylines, keep backgrounds near hero height quiet, and repeat the letters A and B at the last turn. Site marks and captions remain usable in grayscale. The intention follows Riot’s published landmark and silhouette work on Ascent. [Panel references](../REFERENCES.md)
- **Test the screens from both shoulders.** In plan view the Porch-to-center ray passes through the Ledger Screen and the Sill-to-far-ring ray clears the Threshing Bin. In the blockout, put the camera behind Hanuman and Rama at both probe origins. Walk to the edge of the wall, keep the camera on each shoulder in turn and recheck at both outer edges of the disk. Record whether either shoulder can reveal a planter while the viewer’s body remains completely behind cover. The 2D result alone cannot certify either view. [Probe coordinates and cover heights](kurukshetra.json) · [Inferno site-entry and Dust II backdrop studies](../REFERENCES.md)
- **Separate setup time from a free first sighting.** Defenders reach A about 9.9 s ahead of a full-speed outer push. Give one stationary Archive defender that lead, then send an attacker separately from each dry entrance, with teams and shoulders swapped. Mark the first floor point at which each hero becomes visible to the other. If the same stationary hold sees the last covered step of both entrances while remaining hidden from both entrants, move the screen or the Archive mouth before testing the clock. Repeat at B with its 6.0 s arrival advantage.
- **Try to break the retake.** Put Hanuman’s temporary Sena field at the most restrictive entrance on each site. The other dry entrance must still admit a walking player. Test a dropped vessel at each wet/dry seam and at the feet of both B covers, including approach with audio off. Make the first geometry revision at Seal Porch if the long defender A route consistently leaves a second entrant too late to contest.
- **Reuse and provenance.** Instance the project’s roof band for the porch and its UV tile for paving. A small cluster of the existing reed module can dress the shelf from outside collision. The fictional record of field irrigation needs no scriptural text underneath the combat floor. Haryana’s district account associates Kurukshetra with the Mahabharata and names the Saraswati among its rivers. UNESCO’s account of Hampi describes royal enclosures, water structures, pillared halls and open plains in a separate place and period. Those sources inform the choice of shapes and setting. YUGA invents the joint palace, channel, wind register and street network.
