# Lanka · The Wind Arch

From the ferry gate, the two vanes of a signal beacon rise above the western rampart. The lower garden disappears behind the eastern sea wall. Both feed the same salt-damaged circuit. Lighting it could guide a missing ferry home. The gardeners want to keep their water lift working until they know who is out at sea. That disagreement gives the two sites a shared purpose and gives the round-ending shot a human scale. [Round script and endings](../ENCOUNTERS.md)

## Three crossings, one decision

The short A–B crossing runs across the +5 m Wind Arch, down through Fruit Stalls and into Ashoka Garden at +1 m. The bridge parapet is knee-to-waist cover at 1.2 m. The arch opens to Chain Foot from the south and Bell Pier from the north. A team crossing the ridge can meet a defender coming up either ramp. The small kink west of the arch breaks a line along the entire deck. Mark the **Deck crossing** probe for the 23 m stretch the defender should still be able to read.

From Signal Court, the Rain Gallery bends north to Bell Pier. From the pier a descending passage returns to Fruit Stalls. A pair can avoid the Wind Arch while watching the open side of the gallery and the pier exit. The southern salt arcade runs farther out through Jetty Gate, Chain Foot and Slipway. Give that route a continuous roof and a solid seaward wall in the playable blockout. Its concealed early crossing can sell a feint while another player watches the ridge.

## Rain Gallery

The roof follows the 90° east–west line through the new 18 × 14 m room at (−23, 26). The hall footprint is 9 m across and 14 m along that axis. Three bays under a 5 m roof frame a 6 m clear passage. From the western entrance the columns give a runner three short calls before committing to Bell Pier. Keep open sight between columns while screening the carriage at each last bend. The passage from Bell Pier to Fruit Stalls provides a separate descent. It returns to the same garden-side approach as the bridge, so someone who heard “gallery” still needs to see the carrier emerge.

At Sigiriya in Sri Lanka, galleries and stairs mediate the climb to a rock-top palace complex. This is a historical spatial comparison. ## What the builder and players need to see

- **Split beacon, Wind Arch, garden trellis.** Give the beacon a cleft vertical skyline, the bridge a horizontal comb and the garden a scalloped edge. Use the existing watchtower and roof band as editable donors. Keep bright gold, leaves and moving shadows off the carrier's backdrop. Hold the beacon lens steady during combat. Its light can sweep out to sea after the round resolves. [YUGA Blender collections](../../../../../art/reuse-library/README.md)
- **Set the camera at the doorway.** Check both shoulders around Jetty Gate and both directions of the 15 m **Garden entry** probe. A teammate stopped at the last bend must leave space for a second player. Compare at dusk, muted and with reduced effects. Salt and surf ambience should leave captions and confirmed objective cues legible. A bridge crossing gives information to players who can see or locally hear it.
- **First rehearsal.** Call “one crossing Wind, fruit side.” Have the carrier turn back at Chain Foot. Ask the defender to indicate where the carrier is *known* to be. If the entire retreat is visible from one perch, add a solid arcade return near Chain Foot and repeat. Separately record the first arrival at B from Slipway and Garden Wall. Their estimated **2.8 s** difference makes the first damage interrupt worth testing.
