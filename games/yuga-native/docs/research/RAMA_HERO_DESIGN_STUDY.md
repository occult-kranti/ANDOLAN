# Rama: one expressive hero, readable controls

Research snapshot: **17 October 2026 UTC**. Project source inspected: `e765a23264cf3c3c38fab1ded677832a24fdd64e`. This is a design study and implementation handoff; the companion [source ledger](rama-hero-sources.json) records every request, source hash, hero identity, four ability names, token references, and exclusions. Runtime acceptance belongs to the new kit's executed evidence, not this document.

The useful Deadlock lesson is that a small, stable control vocabulary supports complicated *relationships*: aim while moving, choose when to release, exploit a placed object, or spend a commitment window. More hero powers on a selector do not produce that depth. Rama should have one bow attack and four fixed abilities whose spatial forms and cancellation rules can be learned together.

## Six useful comparisons for Rama

1. **Grey Talon — bow input as a continuous action.** Charged Shot accepts a held ability key or Attack; glide and owl control have explicit Mantle exits. The snare can be activated early by the charged shot. This provides precision, positioning, and an interaction between two powers using familiar inputs. Rama should copy the *clarity of the input contract*, with original motion and geometry: draw progression visible on the bow, a single release event, and a clear cancellation path. The corpus's charge/cooldown numbers are not Rama balance recommendations.
2. **Vindicta — aim has a visible opportunity cost.** Flight changes weapon opportunity while Assassinate rewards time spent scoped; partial charge remains meaningful. The useful design question is whether the player can judge “good enough to release” without reading a number. Rama's silhouette, string tension, reticle fill, and brief full-draw cue should agree. A stronger shot should not require a visually frozen body.
3. **Yamato — commitment can remain responsive.** Power Slash permits an early, weaker release. The grappling approach and self-healing slash connect positioning to risk. Rama's early arrow can trade power for timing, while Measured Step provides an explicit escape from an unfinished draw. Do not make every action freely cancellable after damage has committed; that would erase readable counterplay.
4. **Ivy — support changes another player's agency.** Link needs line of sight; carrying an ally constrains their attacks; flight has a damage-related start restriction. Support therefore requires clear targeting, eligibility, and ownership. Guardian Circle should show who benefits and what the benefit actually is. The final implementation may include Rama through a real vitals hook; a decorative shield on an actor without damage reception would be misleading.
5. **Pocket — concurrency is designed, not accidental.** Barrage explicitly survives Air Dash/Flying Cloak, the cloak's recast teleports, and any action ends suitcase hiding. Rama needs a similarly explicit movement/cast compatibility table. Lower-body motion and upper-body aim can coexist; dash, pause, reset, and focus loss must each terminate or preserve the exact same state in combat, animation, and HUD.
6. **Paradox — a coherent spatial grammar supports mastery.** Charge, wall, grenade, and swap reward anticipation of where an enemy will be. They are connected through positioning rather than four unrelated damage buttons. Rama's pierce line, defensive circle, step vector, and volley fan should compose into recognisable decisions: hold a lane, leave a threatened line, reposition for a shot, or punish a predictable approach.

Sinclair (raw complexity 4) and Solomon illustrate the cost of additional state: assistants/copies and persistent movable pieces require explicit ownership, recast meaning, and currently selected object. They are useful advanced contrasts, but that state burden is unnecessary for Rama's first polished kit.

## Why complicated controls can still feel fluent

The available text supports **control consistency** and **explicit state transitions**. It does not prove Deadlock's input latency, animation blending implementation, renderer, or player sentiment. The engineering sources below support a concrete YUGA hypothesis: fluency improves when input, simulation, motion, effects, sound, and UI describe the same event.

- Keep one authority for action acceptance and release. A one-frame pressed edge cannot become three casts because render, physics, and animation each consume it.
- Separate locomotion and action state, with a small compatibility table. Do not enumerate every possible combination of “running while drawing while glowing.”
- Release damage, nocked-arrow disappearance, string recoil, trail birth, and release sound from the same action-instance event. Present impacts from confirmed collision outcomes.
- Blend transitions while preserving contact and aim. More crossfade can hide neither a drifting bow hand nor a sliding planted foot.
- Interpolate presentation between physics ticks; reset interpolation after teleport/reset. Camera aim and character translation need deliberately chosen timing owners. Smoothing must not become uncontrolled input delay.
- Show commitment before large effects: a thin line, bounded ring, vector, or fan. Use shape and timing as well as colour, and keep the target visible beneath the effect.

## Original Rama kit contract and implementation boundary

The settled identifiers are `kodanda`, `piercing_dawn`, `measured_step`, `guardian_circle`, and `dawn_volley`; the implementation owns exact numbers in [rama-ability-kit.json](../../assets/design/rama-ability-kit.json). This study's timings and visual budgets are proposed review targets until measured. The kit is an authored adaptation: the retained [art source library](../../art/production/SOURCE_LIBRARY.md) links Griffith's bow-test passage, lines 8504–8618, at its exact edition. Indigo/gold costume, divine-looking effects, support rules, and these power names are creative choices, not claims that the passage prescribes game mechanics.

**Implementation boundary:** the first kit currently uses a timed 0.18-second Kodanda draw, followed by automatic release on the next 20 Hz tick. Hold-to-charge/early-release damage scaling remains a design proposal, not a completed feature. Default ability bindings are Q, E, C, and X; numbered rows above denote slots, not keyboard bindings.

The intended depth is contextual: step to find a piercing angle; shelter friendly troops while choosing a long draw; force an approach before volleying. It does not require an additional stance selector or a second hidden resource. Do not silently change a learned hold/release action into “press once to select, press again to confirm.” Remapped labels must display the actual binding.

### Simulation and presentation contract

Conceptual event fields (the implementation may choose different names): `action_instance_id`, `action_id`, `simulation_tick`, `phase`, `normalized_progress`, `accepted_aim`, `origin`, `pulse_index`, and optional `target_id`. Phases are `prepare`, `active/release`, `recover`, `cancel`, and `complete`. Effects and animation consume these events; neither decides damage.

| Situation | Required result |
|---|---|
| Normal draw release | Exactly one projectile and one release receipt; nocked arrow disappears on that release. |
| Step during unfinished draw | Draw cancels, string/nock/reticle recover together, zero release damage. |
| Ability attempt during incompatible commitment | Rejected with stable feedback; no cosmetic fake cast and no lost cooldown. |
| Pause, focus loss, reset, title return | No stale held input, delayed phantom release, duplicate pulse, or persistent field from a cleared session. |
| Large frame delta or low render rate | Simulation remains bounded; already scheduled pulses are resolved once and expiry uses simulation time. |
| Reset/teleport | Presentation history resets; no interpolation streak from the old location. |
| Wall or target collision | First collision is authoritative; cosmetic trail terminates consistently with it. |

For a layered rig, locomotion owns pelvis/legs; aim owns a bounded torso/shoulder offset; action overlays own hands/bow during draw/release; contact correction runs with calibrated targets and poles. The Godot TwoBoneIK documentation specifically requires a pole target and warns that intermediate rotations may be ignored when joints are treated as virtual bones. That is a solver contract, not evidence that Rama's grip is correct. Proposed existing project gates remain grip position ≤1.5 cm, grip orientation ≤5°, and planted-foot drift ≤2 cm in explicitly declared contact windows, plus continuous visual review.

### Readability and performance gates

1. Capture continuous idle → strafe → draw → early release → full draw → step cancel → circle → volley → pause/reset at gameplay distance, plus one hand/string close view. Still poses cannot establish continuity.
2. Record accepted-input and release ticks and check their corresponding visual frames. At 60 physics ticks/s, propose acceptance feedback within one tick and release/cue disagreement no greater than one rendered frame; report actual measurements and display cadence.
3. Inspect the same attacks in daylight, night, and a crowded troop fight. Every shape must retain a visible boundary and target; reduce particle opacity/density before adding more bloom. Guardian Circle must not look like a damage ring.
4. Review neutral, draw, step, and landing silhouettes. Metallic ornaments should remain localized; cloth/skin should retain broad readable shading. Keep the face and bow distinguishable from the background.
5. On declared physical hardware/resolution, collect real frame-time median/p95/p99 during both quiet traversal and dense combat, disclose warmup and sample duration, and compare effect counts. A proposed 60 Hz target is a 16.7 ms frame budget; software rendering and accelerated simulation do not establish that target.
