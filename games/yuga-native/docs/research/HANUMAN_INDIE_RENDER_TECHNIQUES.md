# Hanuman rendering studies from Barrow and Blade

Reviewed 25 November 2026 UTC. Beakman Studios’ public project page describes Barrow and Blade as an in-development PC roguelite with a top-down camera. The studio’s short about three optimizations names Steam Deck as a design motivation. The separate shadow breakdown explains a lower-poly second skeletal mesh, vertices projected onto the floor through a material, and sharing the main character’s pose using Unreal’s Leader Pose Component. The studies below use original YUGA artwork and geometry. ## Experiments in the Hanuman trial

### 1. Projected court artwork

Start with a single original wall panel and a cylindrical post. Work from the existing temple kit and packed court materials. Place a Blender camera projector toward the main gameplay angle, author an original illustration, and preserve its fitted UVs when exporting a static mesh. Blender’s UV Project modifier casts from the projector’s negative Z axis. A camera projector follows its perspective or orthographic mode. Blender documents incorrect mapping for vertices behind a perspective projector. Split viewpoints into explicit UV islands and reserve sufficient padding for mipmaps. Inspect the side seam and the end caps on the cylinder. [Existing court](../../art/hanuman/match/README.md) · [Blender 4.2 UV Project](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/modeling/modifiers/modify/uv_project.html) · [Blender glTF exporter](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.3/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html)

Compare the current modeled asset and the projected version at the same placement, lighting and camera path. Review a near approach, a side crossing and a distant lane view. Record visible triangles, material surfaces, texture bytes and render time. Use an opaque material where the shape allows it. A texture supplies surface detail while the mesh provides contact, parallax from its geometry and the actual silhouette. Keep sculpted foreground shapes that carry wall or character identity.

A Blender 4.3.2 smoke fixture produced finite projected UVs on the box and cylinder. A single top projector yielded only four unique UVs for the box’s 24 face corners and 16 for the cylinder’s 96 face corners, exposing coincident projections from that angle. The editable Hanuman court uses face-local UVs and a guarded atlas for its relief layer. [Temple relief source](../../tools/hanuman-match/temple_relief.py)

### 2. Projected ground shadow

Epic documents that sharing a skeletal pose saves Game Thread animation work while the attached mesh components and their sections still contribute Render Thread cost. Reuse Hanuman’s evaluated pose and a simple silhouette proxy for a controlled comparison. First test a floor-aligned opaque or masked proxy on the trial’s flat surface, with shadow casting disabled for the proxy. Scope the feature to the receiver under the character. Where the floor changes height or pitch, measure the receiver from the current support instead of extending one projection behind steps or through the next floor. [Unreal modular characters and Leader Pose](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/working-with-modular-characters-in-unreal-engine) · [Godot spatial shaders](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/spatial_shader.html)

Compare stock shadows, a simple contact footprint and a projected silhouette. Keep lighting fixed while the character idles, turns, runs, jumps, lands and crosses a step or ramp. A projected silhouette should remain aligned with the posed feet, have a stable edge at a grazing camera angle, and avoid rendering above a lower level or across an unrelated surface. Check clipping, z-fighting and overlapping dark shapes in a crowd. For stock shadow maps, *Peter Panning* names shadow detachment caused by excessive depth bias. A floating projected proxy can look similar. Its separation comes from the chosen receiver or geometric offset. [Microsoft shadow-map guide](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/dxtecharts/common-techniques-to-improve-shadow-depth-maps) · [Unreal directional-light properties](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/directional-lights-in-unreal-engine)

The Hanuman trial’s first comparison uses a single feathered footprint on the measured limestone floor. It keeps the animated sun shadow, dims the footprint when the sun shadow is active, and fades near the end of its vertical range and the receiver’s edge. This establishes a portable baseline for a separate pose-projection experiment. [Trial contact shadow](../../scripts/hanuman_contact_shadow.gd)

### 3. Shared ground-effect texture

Use the existing Hanuman presentation events to prototype one texture sampled by a floor material. Start with Lanka Dahan’s lingering appearance. In a bounded floor region, write normalized effect age or timestamp and a separate effect kind or intensity into a data texture. Define which effect wins at overlap or allocate separate channels per type. Use the same clock for stamping and decay, clear data on the established generation reset, and keep appearance on the existing pause and reduced-effects controls. Keep the existing authoritative cast geometry, hit timing and warning outline. Check the edge of the receiver and floor changes before applying the technique to wider map areas. [Hanuman design](../HANUMAN_GAME_DESIGN.md) · [Presentation event receiver](../../scripts/hanuman_trial_effects.gd) · [Godot ImageTexture updates](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_imagetexture.html#class-imagetexture-method-update)

Use a fixed-size texture and reuse the allocation. Godot’s `ImageTexture.update()` expects unchanged dimensions, format and mipmap configuration. A SubViewport is another documented way to render to a texture and adds a viewport update to profile. Compare the current effect geometry with the shared-floor version under one cast, overlapping casts and the current capacity. Check cast cancellation, pause, expiry, reset, renderer readback and the effect of clearing or uploading the texture. Unreal’s `DrawMaterialToRenderTarget` documentation explains the cost of repeatedly binding the target and recommends a Begin/End Canvas draw pair for multiple primitives to one target. [Godot SubViewport](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_subviewport.html) · [Unreal Draw Material to Render Target](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/BlueprintAPI/Rendering/DrawMaterialtoRenderTarget/?application_version=5.3)

The trial’s first data-texture pass uses three 96 × 96 regions in one RG32F atlas. The fields store local arrival time and remaining span. Their shared MultiMesh material receives one clock for steady decay. One whole atlas upload contains 216 KiB of pixel data, excluding engine and driver overhead. [Trial field source](../../scripts/hanuman_ground_field.gd) · [Trial field shader](../../shaders/hanuman_ground_field.gdshader)

## Results in Hanuman’s practice court

- **Projected architecture.** The Blender court uses a packed 512 × 512 atlas with four original carvings across 302 panels. The prior scene had 110,690 court triangles and the new scene has 86,654. The secondary view shows how the walls turn the corner. [Carving study and camera views](../../art/hanuman/match/temple-relief-study.png) · [Scene build counts](../../art/hanuman/match/build-report.json)
- **Ground contact.** Both shadow preferences passed twelve checks each through the practice launcher. In a matched 960 × 540 Forward+ software-rendered pair, 976 pixels exceeded the recorded darkening threshold, all beneath the feet. [Control frame](hanuman-indie-runtime/shadow-control.png) · [Contact frame](hanuman-indie-runtime/shadow-contact.png) · [Method and measured difference](hanuman-indie-runtime/shadow-verification.json)
- **Timed field.** The three-tile atlas occupies 221,184 bytes. The lifecycle fixture recorded nine uploads and zero uploads on clock-only updates. Twenty-three headless checks and twenty-four Forward+ software-rendered checks passed. The actual frame shows Mountain Pulse 0.24 s after impact. ## Measure the actual renderer

A shared floor shader can remove extra surface submissions for individual marks while still performing texture writes, sampling and shading. A projected mesh adds a visible mesh and skinning work. Material surfaces, shadow and depth passes, texture updates and transparency can change the total. Godot’s MultiMesh batches instances of one mesh per pass, with visibility applied at the MultiMesh level. Godot Decal is an alternative on Forward+ and Mobile, with a per-mesh limit on Mobile and no support in Compatibility. Use the RendererServer frame counters and the visual profiler together. Log the delta in draw calls, visible primitives, CPU and GPU frame times, texture bytes and update frequency for each otherwise-identical replay. [Godot MultiMesh](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/using_multimesh.html) · [Godot Decal](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_decal.html) · [RenderingServer counters](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_renderingserver.html#class-renderingserver-constant-rendering-info-total-draw-calls-in-frame) · [Godot Visual Profiler](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/scripting/debug/debugger_panel.html#visual-profiler)

Valve’s OLED specification lists a 1280 × 800 screen, a 4–15 W APU with 8 RDNA 2 compute units and 16 GB on-board memory. Record the actual model, SteamOS version, renderer, render scale, power setting and any frame cap for a handheld claim. A cloud or laptop run can establish correctness and local frame costs. Sustained handheld performance requires a physical Steam Deck run of the same matchup, with consistent settings and a stable warmed scene. [Valve Steam Deck tech specs](https://www.steamdeck.com/en/tech)
