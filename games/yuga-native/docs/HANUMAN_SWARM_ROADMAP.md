# Hanuman production roadmap

Run two panels of two people in each production round. The author makes one bounded change. The peer opens that exact artifact, tries to disprove its acceptance claim, and takes the next edit when the author hands over the files. They swap again on the next round. The lead owns shared interfaces, the render queue, integration, and published evidence.

This round pairs the character artist with the technical animator on the Blender handoff. The combat designer and independent verifier are paired on reusable combat presentation and browser delivery. Each panel has one writer per file at a time. Both panels can inspect, test and prepare work together. Blender, Godot imports and software-render capture use the available CPU in queued slots.

| Round | Panel A | Panel B | What closes the round |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Character fidelity | Sculpt the face, torso and garment silhouettes from the generated Hanuman key art. Peer retopologizes and checks all views against the existing scale and shoulders. | Build the bronze, fur, cloth and eye materials from the existing node layout. Peer calibrates neutral and dusk light, then tests at gameplay distance. | Matched front, rear, close and actual game views. Keep separate decisions for face, cloth, metal, tail and silhouette. |
| 2 · Motion and contact | Author weighted stance, gait, vault, landing and turn transitions. Peer tunes the four-joint response on top of the accepted clips. | Author separate movement and attack reviews for gada, palm guard, tail sweep and mountain cast. Peer checks grip, feet, fabric and tail contact by scrubbing across release and cancellation. | A continuous Blender reel and actual native motion. Verified weapon grip, no unintended root travel, bounded tail response and clean pause, interruption and return to idle. |
| 3 · Effects and sound | Author readable cast and impact forms from the profile and sockets. Peer tests range and angle, partial obstruction, reduced effects and close camera. | Develop guard, windup and impact cues using the existing audio bus. Peer verifies the timing against live event receipts and listens for level and overlap. | Native playback shows each ability from warning to recovery. Cast effects stay within gameplay selection bounds. Sound timing and listening have separate evidence. |
| 4 · Map and camera | Reuse the courtyard material and collision kit to build one original Kishkindha encounter. Peer checks sightlines, camera occlusion, vault geometry and traversal at the active hero scale. | Extend the title and trial entry using the existing launcher and theme. Peer improves the selected-hero preview, keyboard and mouse behavior, text sizing, cancellation and recovery. | Actual normal launch, hero selection and play. Matched views prove readability under day and dusk lighting, at spawn and during movement. |
| 5 · Performance and delivery | Add explicit hero and effect LODs. Peer profiles representative captures at multiple camera distances and records physical GPU timing when available. | Package the editable Blender asset, native model, cues, and separate browser asset. Peer tests missing or slow asset delivery, reconnect for online work, and package readback. | Reopen and play the exact package. Check that graphics do not pop, that budget changes preserve pose/contact, and that each platform claim is backed by execution. |

These are the next production gates. The five Hanuman prototype passes already completed have their own [decision and evidence record](research/hanuman-nov25/PANEL.md). Future panels can repeat the schedule for a different hero with a new art brief and an explicit ability profile.

| Element | Share across panels | Duplicate or author per hero, map or platform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Base body, pose and bind | Use the existing CC0 anatomy, Blender skeleton naming, rest transforms and action library as the starting kit. Keep one source rig until an incompatible anatomy needs a new rig family. | Sculpt a distinct head, proportions, garment and silhouette. Export its own GLB. Bake packed Blender files for handoff when the recipient needs to open them without link paths. |
| Tail, hair and garment motion | Reuse `hero_chain_response.gd` and its validated chain profile format. It consumes swept movement and adds bounded rotation after the authored pose. | Give each appendage a real weighted chain and its own constraints, axis and response profile. Hanuman’s four links and authored tail strike belong to Hanuman. |
| Animation, props and sockets | Share common locomotion and timing conventions within compatible rigs. Give the artist, effects and rig panels one socket naming contract. | Build hero-specific weapon contact, timing and recovery clips. Keep gada and tail tip locators bound to this rig. Artist review decides which reused base actions need new keyframes. |
| Materials and textures | Share the UV and color-space conventions, procedural nodes for wear and normal detail, and named color inputs. Reuse the court’s surface finish and production lighting under exact scene names. | Keep generated and painted color maps specific to their surface. Adapt the madder material to another cloth only if its weave scale, palette and rights fit. Preserve source reference images separately from shipping textures. |
| Combat and effects | Use the existing 20 Hz practice receiver, geometry functions, accepted cast event format, ribbon shaders and pooled audio. Reuse their actual tests. | Put action timing, cooldowns, damage, ranges, motion cues and presentation choices in separate profiles. Capture each hero’s silhouette and impact against its own contract. |
| Terrain, props and lighting | Link existing court stone, columns, lanterns and materials as building pieces. Reuse collision rules and camera tests. | Compose an original arena, certify collision and routes for that layout, and package distinct lighting presets when the map demands them. |
| UI, input and delivery | Share the title theme, scene transition, remapping and recovery paths. Use the selected hero’s asset as the critical load. Test a failed request and an interrupted entry. | Provide hero identity, iconography and labels. Keep distinct native and browser resource budgets. Validate desktop, touch and network claims on their own entry paths. |
| Evidence and source tracking | Reuse the source-hashed launch driver, Blender shot-list format, movie verifier and license notices. Feed different plans to the same tools. | Save one exact receipt and originals per accepted build. Keep rejected comparisons if they explain a choice. Large duplicated authoring files belong in delivery archives when there is no edit or test need for a second Git copy. |

Each author hands over the source path and hash, tool and version, affected interfaces, exact command, original evidence, failure found, change made, and remaining limit. The peer verifies the pin, reruns a case that could fail, and adds the revised artifact or documents the acceptance limit. The lead checks the normal entry path and ships the smallest bundle that another panel can open and reproduce.

Keep rig roles, socket roles, ability phases and material slots in separate manifests as compatibility grows. A team can then reuse a named action while another team changes only the material for a specific camera. The current Hanuman GLB contains fourteen image entries with ten unique byte payloads. A future packaging pass can test shared storage for the equal payloads after checking color space, normal decoding and material slot behavior. The current capture identifies the fourteen extracted files by their original bytes.

The team can use the installed OpenCode Game Studios `art-bible`, `team-combat` and `perf-profile` references with the repo’s YUGA hero, motion and evidence skills. For browser systems, verify local module behavior first and then use documented, version-pinned examples.
