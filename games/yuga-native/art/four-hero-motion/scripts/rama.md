# Rama · the held horizon

The most important line in Rama’s silhouette runs from the right elbow through the bow grip, past the nock and along the arrow. In this rig **the right hand holds the bow and the left hand draws**. The thumb, fingers and eyes should make that readable before adding trails or flashes.

## Rig, clocks and hand contacts

The enhanced Confluence browser rig uses the 164-joint far model and nineteen animation clips. Nine are embedded in the base GLB: `idle`, `run`, `jump`, `defeat`, `fire`, `cast`, `cast1`, `cast2` and `cast3`. The packer adds ten staged clips from the evaluated native controller. The closest reproducible export comes from that cache, including the recorded motion and actual muzzle. The native Godot controller also has locomotion, traversal and reactions that the enhanced browser state machine does not currently select. [Native far rig](../../../assets/heroes/rama-hero-far.glb) · [Pose cache and packing](../../../../yuga-rust/tools/pack_rama.py) · [Export and pose verification](../../../docs/rust-rebuild/deep-search/RAMA-PORT.md)

All **F** references are 60 fps review frames since accepted input, with F0 at acceptance. A gameplay tick is three review frames. Clip durations in seconds can be fractions of a tick. The Rust release is the first 20 Hz tick at or beyond the authored windup. Unlock is the first tick at or beyond the sum of windup and recovery. A correct review can therefore show the final sub-frame of the drawn pose sampled at the authoritative release boundary. The capture bakes the native draw and release separately. Each clip starts at its own frame zero. [Timing code](../../../../yuga-rust/src/rama.rs) · [Native action values](../../../assets/design/rama-ability-kit.json)

The native bow grip starts at local offset (0, 0.105, 0.025) on `wrist.R`. The draw contact is (0.012613, 0.114969, −0.042728) in `wrist.L` space. Constrain the `bowstring` marker to this contact at full draw. The projectile is born at the sampled muzzle in actor-local coordinates, about (−0.085, 1.561, 0.629) metres before the actor’s world rotation and translation. Align the shaft to the renderer’s nock-to-grip direction during windup. Hide the nocked shaft when the real projectile takes over. Side-view slow motion should show one arrow. The frame-level posing, gaze, cloth delays and shot recommendations below are **proposed authoring direction**. Existing clip names, state handoffs and gameplay markers are identified separately. The current exported stages were captured standing on level ground. A moving or pitch-adjusted runtime pose needs its own contact review.

A cinematic insert may use a profile of the left-hand cheek contact, then a wider shot that keeps target and string on screen. Ordinary combat cameras should retain target visibility. ## Classic Sangam coverage

In Sangam, the primary is an ordinary ranged attack using the compact rig’s `fire` action. Q **Steady Arrow** uses `cast1` and an aimed bolt. E **Measured Step** uses `cast2` and dashes up to 10 m. R **Dawn Volley** uses `cast3` and winds up for one second, then releases **three arrows together** on the locked aim. For that rendition, peak the left drawing hand near F60 and give one release silhouette spanning the three rays. The classic rig has nine clips, with separate upper-body playback when available. Preserve the named variant in every storyboard and review video so the classic release does not overwrite the enhanced stagger. ## Review shots

1. Capture both hand sockets and the nock at F11/F12/F13 for Kodanda and F38/F39/F40 for Piercing Dawn. Inspect from side and shoulder under low and high aim. Include an occluder close to the muzzle and inspect the first real projectile position.
2. At run speed, shoot a primary while the near foot is planted. Look for finger-through-string artifacts, lower-body freezing or foot drift. Inspect brow and cheek clipping on both sides of the string. The existing motion cache proves a standing sample only.
3. In a two-team rehearsal, pause at F32/F33/F51 and at actual field expiry. Compare the ring’s fixed origin, the caster’s free movement and the shield and health of friend and foe. For Dawn Volley, capture F56–70 at reduced speed with separated targets and visible cover, counting three departures.
4. Test hit, cancellation, defeat and respawn through their actual native states. After a manual reload, verify the primary visually reopens at F96. Log the LOD, state, camera and input with each take. Review contact and facial acting with a human viewer alongside the independent packed joint checks.
