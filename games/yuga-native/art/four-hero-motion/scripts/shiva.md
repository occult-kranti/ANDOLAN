# Shiva · character animation script

Shiva is an original area-control hero proposal for YUGA. He makes one strong movement and lets the space around it settle. The trident presents a rigid vertical line. His cloth and tied drum keep moving for a few frames after the hand stops. This difference should carry the character at match distance.

The classic game ships 14 other heroes. Shiva has no entry in its roster or existing per-hero browser GLB. This brief supplies names, times and staging for new animation assets. Its proposed numeric balance needs playtesting and a new authoritative cast definition before the actions can run in matches. An unknown hero ID currently falls back to Rama, so a successful spawn alone cannot qualify this hero. ## Identity, proportion and sockets

- Build the readable pose from an open left side and a tall trishula in the right hand. Three continuous prongs should remain distinct against sky and ground. A small hourglass damaru attaches below the head of the shaft. Mark separate sockets at the grip, center tip, drum waist, two beaters and both soles. The weapon never changes hands in this set.
- A crescent and a bound crest of matted hair make the head recognizable in profile. Keep the silver crescent proud of the hair and clear of the trident outlines. Separate warm copper and cool ash-blue by value. A third-eye mark remains visible in a neutral close-up. One left-side loose lock and a narrow sash supply enough motion around the face.
- Use two visible arms for the playable silhouette. The Chola Nataraja bronzes provide a reference for a lifted leg, the standing foot and the arc around the body. Keep the stance, gaze and beat original to this animation. A split arc lit in the arena is a gameplay signal. The museum describes the ring and dance within Shiva's religious iconography. ## Match actions

Author at 30 fps. F0 is the accepted command. Durations below measure the interval from F0 to the end marker. The first three actions use existing attack shapes. The ultimate requires a new delayed circular release. Frame markers cue presentation and never award damage. ## Acting, effects and review

1. Capture front, rear three-quarter and shaft-side views at F0, F11, F23, F24 and F34 of the ultimate. The left sole, both hands and the middle tine should remain distinguishable at ordinary match size. The camera-side elbow may move outward if it hides the face.
2. At portrait distance, the gaze lands before the head on Q. On R, the lower lids narrow while the upper lip stays relaxed; the breath releases at foot plant. Give the third-eye mark a short value change from F16 to F24, with a steady crescent contour in reduced-effects mode.
3. Inspect palm-to-shaft contact and prong separation across every frame. Review finger intersections and beater overshoot with the effects pass off. During a run-and-fire blend, compare left and right knee phase against uninterrupted `run`. A wide recovery should never sweep through the near camera.
4. Replay an accepted R, death at F23, leaving the circle before F24, and reconnecting during F16. The reconnecting actor should seek to elapsed cast time. A duplicate receipt must never restart the stomp. Resolve hit audio at the authoritative damage event and preserve the ground outline without audio.
5. Deliver one beauty take in neutral light and one flat silhouette take from the real third-person camera. Show the left-foot plant against a fixed floor grid. The scripts describe proposed animations; state registration, kit balance, a skinned export and in-engine captures require their own evidence.
