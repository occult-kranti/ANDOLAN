# Hanuman · keeper of the front line

Hanuman carries the gada in his **right hand**. His left hand distinguishes an offer of protection from a strike. His tail shows intention before the opponent has to read the particles. Stage a movement so that a rival can read the hand, the planted foot and the direction of danger from the ordinary rear three-quarter camera.

## Rig, clocks and shot convention

The enhanced Confluence character has 167 skin joints. Use the authored Blender actions or the Rust export that incorporates the native Godot overrides. The base GLB contains earlier versions of the same five clip names. The portable rig has eleven clips: `idle`, `run`, `jump`, `defeat`, `cast`, `cast1`, `cast2`, `cast3`, `fire`, `tail_sweep` and `mountain_pulse`. The enhanced five-action kit uses the last two custom clips, `fire`, `cast1` and `cast2`. Generic `cast` and `cast3` remain available in the asset. The compact Sangam game has its own nine-clip model and classic kit. All **F** labels use a 60 fps review clock, starting at F0 when the cast is accepted. One simulation tick spans three review frames. If a Blender shot begins at frame 1, add one to the labels below. The runtime seeks to authored normalized markers. Multiplying a review frame by a clip’s source FPS would put a fast swing in the wrong pose. Use the accepted tick and place foot contact under the capsule. Local +Z is forward in game, +Y is up. The Blender authoring script uses −Y forward and Z up. Body-part offsets are review targets for the indicated shot. They do not add gameplay contacts. `tail_01` through `tail_04` are deform joints. Use the `tail_tip` node as a measurement point. R **Mountain Pulse** uses `cast3` with the classic Mountain Descent mechanic. F0–42 winds up. F42–69 moves the body on an arc toward the locked target. Use a visible ally health change for the heal accent, and leave the marker legible when the opponent steps out. The classic rig has no dedicated `tail_sweep` clip.
