# Devnagar visual assets

Every visual asset in `index.ts` is original project material, authored for ANDOLAN in this repository. The renderer uses PlayCanvas primitive meshes and locally generated HTML canvas textures. There are no downloaded game assets, extracted binaries, third-party characters, music, logos, map tiles, or reference-game textures.

The authored Old Market district is approximately 200 × 200 metres. Its streets and building footprints are imported from the authoritative simulation. Garage, newsroom, clinic, public archive, mill depot, community radio, assembly square, public hearing portico and river overlook are purpose-built architectural compositions. Signs use invented in-world businesses and local browser sans-serif lettering. Clothing, vehicles, road patina, plaster, windows, foliage and props are procedural original geometry and colors.

The visual direction uses desaturated teal, turmeric, terracotta and aged cream; rain-dark asphalt; warm shop windows; mature street trees; rooftop water tanks; awnings; overhead utility wires; and a monsoon-evening sky. The beta refinement adds a cool slate sky above an amber horizon, low warm sunlight, painted shop plinths, shutters, selected balconies and roof terraces. All details remain original authored geometry. This is a fictional city, not a reproduction of an actual location.

`createWorld(canvas)` returns a rendering-only view. `update(snapshot, playerId, dt)` interpolates server-owned player and vehicle transforms and follows the active player. Decorative pedestrians have bounded visual-only pavement loops. The renderer never grants progress, changes collision state or determines mission completion. The camera performs segment/AABB obstruction tests against authoritative buildings and interior walls. `setQuality`, `setComfort`, `setOverview` and `setCamera` configure presentation. The browser performs rendering; a cloud GPU is unnecessary.

The original `remote-poses.ts` helper buffers remote player and vehicle transforms for a 100 ms presentation delay. Local player and occupied-vehicle transforms bypass that buffer. Remote samples apply directly rather than receiving a second smoothing delay; server-owned seats, existence, contracts and progress always use the latest snapshot. Entity identity, seat changes and large discontinuities prevent interpolation across unrelated poses. Missing future data holds the newest position rather than inventing movement. `resetInterpolation()` clears history on welcome/restart. This is presentation smoothing, not rollback, prediction, lag compensation or a network reliability guarantee.

`setObjectiveTarget(position)` follows a server-owned free-roam contract target, including a moving recovery vehicle. Optional `radius` and `kind` fields display the actual usable radius and a camera-facing WALK, PARK or PASS plaque. The ring texture's visible edge is calibrated to the supplied radius; it sits above the authored terrace floors. Passing `null` hides the destination; `undefined` resumes Story's original compact lantern marker. These settings change presentation only. In free roam, authoritative collective contribution thresholds produce four workshop bulbs, existing clinic supply crates, and two civic pennant meshes. Story evidence and assembly visuals remain separate from free-roam contributions.

The opening camera faces Yusuf and the garage entrance. Original contact figures and nameplates identify the workshop, newsroom, clinic, archive, depot, radio, assembly, hearing and river engineer. The felt board begins empty; up to six record summaries and comparison threads appear from the authoritative evidence count. Replacement clinic crates appear after the delivery acknowledgment, and a bounded group of six residents attends the later assembly. These are visual reflections of authored mission state. They do not infer real political behavior.

The amber destination lantern was implemented as original procedural geometry after reviewing the project’s generated art-direction sheet. That image is a concept target, not a screenshot of runtime fidelity. The runtime’s lantern, pinned records, workshop props and overhead cables retain the stylized primitive-mesh approach.

Static architecture and moving citizen/vehicle parts use PlayCanvas batches. Low graphics renders at 60% CSS resolution, disables shadow maps and rain, and shows four stationary background pedestrians. Comfort mode stops ambient pedestrian motion and lantern sway. This improves headroom on constrained clients but does not establish a mobile or public-server performance capacity.

The beta graphics use locally drawn proportional sign textures, glazing gradients, toolboard silhouettes, drain ironwork and pennant fabric. Repeated identical signs share materials. Road drains are flat surface details; bins sit beside existing sidewalk furniture; garage beams and tools stay on the shell. No additional collision geometry or blocked entrances are introduced. The compact cabin is an original five-face trapezoidal mesh with a cream roof, round headlights and satin trim. Brighter player jackets, courier bags and light shoe soles distinguish playable figures from decorative residents without inventing online users. Soft contact shades ground players and vehicles even when low quality disables directional shadows. These are stylized painted shadows and glazing, not dynamic reflections or new physics.

The follow camera keeps the same vehicle heading while reversing, limits automatic rotation to 120 degrees per second (45 in Comfort), and gives manual orbit priority for two seconds (four in Comfort). Entering or exiting blends the camera anchor over 0.4 seconds and eases the viewing distance; entry alignment begins while the vehicle is parked and yields to manual orbit. The avatar appears immediately at the server-selected safe exit position. During steady movement the camera translates with the rendered actor, avoiding additional layers of positional follow lag. `getCameraYaw()` returns the displayed camera's bearing so walking remains relative to the view; `recenterCamera()` smoothly returns behind the current actor. Obstruction checks constrain the final smoothed camera, so approaching a wall brings the camera inward immediately while release eases outward. Leaving the title overview initializes the follow target to the active actor. World projection returns CSS pixels independently of graphics quality or display density.

Two small original wall posters introduce the fictional Republic Times satire sheet: “STILL PENDING / NOW TRENDING” in the newsroom and “REQUEST A FORM / TO REQUEST A FORM” at community radio. Their wording, textures and layout are authored in this repository. They do not use a real outlet's logo, extracted artwork or real-person likenesses, and they add no collision geometry.

Limitations: primitive-based character animation and vehicle models; authored building shells with selected open interiors; no facial animation, voice performances, full weather simulation, physically simulated traffic, or distant district streaming. Water and wet road highlights are stylized, not ray-traced reflections. Performance depends on the player's device and should be tested before increasing district density or room capacity.
