# Devnagar visual assets

Every visual asset in `index.ts` is original project material, authored for ANDOLAN in this repository. The renderer uses PlayCanvas primitive meshes and locally generated HTML canvas textures. There are no downloaded game assets, extracted binaries, third-party characters, music, logos, map tiles, or reference-game textures.

The authored Old Market district is approximately 200 × 200 metres. Its streets and building footprints are imported from the authoritative simulation. Garage, newsroom, clinic, public archive, mill depot, community radio, assembly square, public hearing portico and river overlook are purpose-built architectural compositions. Signs use invented in-world businesses and local browser sans-serif lettering. Clothing, vehicles, road patina, plaster, windows, foliage and props are procedural original geometry and colors.

The visual direction uses desaturated teal, turmeric, terracotta and aged cream; rain-dark asphalt; warm shop windows; mature street trees; rooftop water tanks; awnings; overhead utility wires; and a monsoon-evening sky. This is a fictional city, not a reproduction of an actual location.

`createWorld(canvas)` returns a rendering-only view. `update(snapshot, playerId, dt)` interpolates server-owned player and vehicle transforms and follows the active player. Decorative pedestrians have bounded visual-only pavement loops. The renderer never grants progress, changes collision state or determines mission completion. The camera performs segment/AABB obstruction tests against authoritative buildings and interior walls. `setQuality`, `setComfort`, `setOverview` and `setCamera` configure presentation. The browser performs rendering; a cloud GPU is unnecessary.

The opening camera faces Yusuf and the garage entrance. Original contact figures and nameplates identify the workshop, newsroom, clinic, archive, depot, radio, assembly, hearing and river engineer. The felt board begins empty; up to six record summaries and comparison threads appear from the authoritative evidence count. Replacement clinic crates appear after the delivery acknowledgment, and a bounded group of six residents attends the later assembly. These are visual reflections of authored mission state. They do not infer real political behavior.

The amber destination lantern was implemented as original procedural geometry after reviewing the project’s generated art-direction sheet. That image is a concept target, not a screenshot of runtime fidelity. The runtime’s lantern, pinned records, workshop props and overhead cables retain the stylized primitive-mesh approach.

Static architecture and moving citizen/vehicle parts use PlayCanvas batches. Low graphics renders at 60% CSS resolution, disables shadow maps and rain, and shows four stationary background pedestrians. Comfort mode stops ambient pedestrian motion and lantern sway. This improves headroom on constrained clients but does not establish a mobile or public-server performance capacity.

The follow camera keeps the same vehicle heading while reversing, limits automatic rotation to 120 degrees per second (45 in Comfort), and gives manual orbit priority for two seconds (four in Comfort). Obstruction checks constrain the final smoothed camera, so approaching a wall brings the camera inward immediately while release eases outward. Leaving the title overview initializes the follow target to the active actor. World projection returns CSS pixels independently of graphics quality or display density.

Limitations: primitive-based character animation and vehicle models; authored building shells with selected open interiors; no facial animation, voice performances, full weather simulation, physically simulated traffic, or distant district streaming. Water and wet road highlights are stylized, not ray-traced reflections. Performance depends on the player's device and should be tested before increasing district density or room capacity.
